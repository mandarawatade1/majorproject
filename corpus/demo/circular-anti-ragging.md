## Circular DEMO/ADMIN/2026/05

**To:** All students, with particular attention to the incoming first year
**From:** Anti-Ragging Committee
**Subject:** What ragging is, and how to report it

## What ragging is

Ragging is any conduct by a student, whether by words spoken or written or by
an act, which has the effect of teasing, treating or handling another student
with rudeness; indulging in rowdy or indisciplined activities which cause
annoyance, hardship or psychological harm; or raising fear or apprehension in a
fresher.

It includes acts framed as tradition, introduction, or fun. The framing does
not change what it is. Consent obtained under peer pressure is not consent.

It includes conduct occurring off campus, online, and in group chats.

## What it is not

Ordinary academic interaction between seniors and juniors, mentoring, and
organised orientation activities run by the institute are not ragging. Students
should not hesitate to approach seniors for legitimate academic help.

## Reporting

A report may be made to: any member of the Anti-Ragging Committee, the Head of
the concerned department, the student's assigned mentor, or the administrative
office. Any of these routes is sufficient; a student does not need to find the
"right" one.

A report may be made by the affected student, or by a witness. Witness reports
are acted on.

## Confidentiality

The identity of a reporting student is not disclosed to the students
complained against during the inquiry. Records are held by the Committee.

Students should be aware that where a matter falls under statutory obligations,
the institute may be required to involve external authorities, and the
Committee will say so plainly rather than implying that everything stays
internal.

## What happens after a report

The Committee acknowledges the report, conducts an inquiry in which all parties
are heard, and records a finding with reasons. Interim measures — such as
separating the parties in laboratory or hostel allocation — may be taken while
the inquiry proceeds.

## Undertakings

Students and parents submit the prescribed anti-ragging undertakings at
admission. Submitting the undertaking is an administrative step; it is not a
substitute for understanding what the undertaking says.

## For the incoming year

If something happens and you are unsure whether it counts, report it and let
the Committee decide. Under-reporting because a student was unsure is the
failure mode this circular exists to prevent.
